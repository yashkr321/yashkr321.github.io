Building Anethix Labs From the Ground Up: What I Learned as a Co-Founder

2026-09-20 · 5 min read
startups leadership anethix labs

I was one of the Co-Founders of Anethix Labs Private Limited, and my involvement with the company started before Anethix Labs was even legally incorporated.

In the beginning, there was no established company, no finished product, and no large team. There was simply an idea and a small group of people trying to turn that idea into something real.

From the early stages, I was responsible for handling the technical side of Anethix Labs. I worked on the product, development, technical architecture and the different decisions involved in turning an idea into a working software product.

That period gave me my first real experience of what it means to build something from the ground up.

Building Before the Company Was Official

One of the most interesting parts of my experience with Anethix Labs was that the work started before the company itself was formally incorporated.

We were already discussing the product, working on the technical side and trying to figure out how everything would come together.

Later, Anethix Labs Private Limited was formally incorporated, but by then I had already spent considerable time working on the technical foundation of what we were trying to build.

Being involved from that early stage taught me that building a company is very different from simply having an idea for one.

An idea is easy to discuss.

Turning it into something usable requires time, experimentation, technical work and a lot of decisions that aren't obvious at the beginning.

My Role at Anethix Labs

As a Co-Founder, my primary contribution was on the technology and product-development side.

I was involved in developing the product and dealing with the technical problems that came with building it from scratch. There were many things that had to be figured out along the way — what to build, how to build it, what technologies to use and how different parts of the product should work together.

It wasn't a large engineering organisation with clearly separated teams and responsibilities.

It was a small startup environment where you often had to figure things out yourself.

That was probably one of the most valuable parts of the experience.

I wasn't simply working on an assigned task. I had to think about the product as a whole and understand how technical decisions affected what we were trying to build as a company.

What I Learned About Startups

My biggest takeaway from Anethix Labs wasn't a particular programming language or technology.

It was understanding how much people and communication matter when building something together.

Technology can solve many problems, but it cannot solve a lack of communication or trust between people.

There were good periods, difficult periods, disagreements and mistakes. Looking back, I think all of them were useful in their own way because they showed me what working in a startup environment actually looks like.

One lesson that stayed with me is simple:

“A title doesn't make someone a leader.”

Leadership becomes visible when things become difficult, when people disagree, and when decisions don't go as planned. It is reflected in how people communicate, how they treat one another and whether they take responsibility.

It Wasn't a Huge Startup Story

I don't want to present Anethix Labs as some massive startup success story. It wasn't.

It was a small company and an early-stage attempt to build a product. But I don't think an experience has to become a huge success to be valuable.

For me, the value was in getting to experience the entire process from the beginning — starting with an idea, working on the technology, building a product, working with other people and learning from the things that didn't go as expected.

Those are experiences that are difficult to learn from a classroom alone.

Moving Forward

My time actively working on Anethix Labs eventually came to an end, but the experience has stayed with me.

It changed how I look at software development, startups, teamwork and leadership.

More importantly, it gave me a much clearer understanding of the kind of environment I want to work in and the kind of person I want to become when building something with others.

I'm grateful that I got the opportunity to experience Anethix Labs from its earliest days.

It wasn't perfect, and there are things I would do differently today.

But that's probably the point.

You don't need every experience to become a success story.

Sometimes, building something from scratch, seeing what works, seeing what doesn't, and learning from the people around you is valuable enough.

— Yash Kumar Gupta

Co-Founder, Anethix Labs
